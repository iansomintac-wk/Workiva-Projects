OSM Book of Business Tracker — version notes (extracted verbatim from the tracker file header)

CHANGELOG:
  v97 - Three leaks that only a full book could show.

    Everything fixed in this file for the last ten versions was about EMPTINESS:
    surfaces that rendered nothing because nothing had been logged. Putting the
    fully-populated demo book through the whole-book purity guard tested the
    opposite condition for the first time, and found three writes nobody asked
    for.

    1. getSessionNote() CREATED A RECORD ON READ. renderSessionCard() calls it
       once per session in the plan, so merely opening the Session Plan tab
       stamped eight empty note records onto the account. This is the third member
       of the family - getDayClose() at v90, getGainsightQueue() at v95 - and the
       worst of the three, because it fires on a RENDER rather than on a question
       somebody asked. Now split: readSessionNote() returns a throwaway default
       and creates nothing; getSessionNote() still materialises, and only writers
       call it. The three read-only call sites were switched over.

    2. THE SUITE CONSUMED THE BASELINE IT WAS MEASURING. getWeeklyDigest() re-bases
       the weekly snapshot once it is seven days old. That is correct behaviour on
       a render - the digest is meant to re-base weekly - and wrong when
       runSelfChecks() triggers it, because the "since last week" diff is silently
       destroyed and cannot be recovered. Guarded on _suiteRunning, the same
       reasoning as the v82 save guard: a diagnostic may read anything and must
       change nothing. Real renders still re-base on schedule.

    3. A CHECK NOW NAMES THE FAMILY. Three getters of the same shape were found in
       three separate versions, each by accident, each only visible on a book that
       had never touched the field in question. The new check asserts all three
       are read-only, so a fourth is caught by the suite rather than by luck.

    Verified on BOTH books: 302/302 on the FIRST run against a freshly seeded
    17-account demo and against the real 37-account book, with the weekly snapshot
    and every session-note map byte-identical afterwards.

    Four checks, 298 -> 302.

  v96 - The first time the AI layer actually ran.

    Every AI feature in this file had been built, self-checked and never once
    executed against a real provider. The checks asserted the plumbing and the
    failure paths; nothing had ever asserted the OUTPUT, because there was none.

    Run for the first time against a fully-populated 17-account book, with the
    user's own key, it found three things no amount of reading the code would
    have surfaced:

    1. NAMES ON SCREEN, IDS ON THE WIRE. The generated paragraph read "BEST0 has
       2 unanswered attempts" while the card immediately beneath it read "Aldridge
       Energy" - and the id-to-name mapping was folded away inside a <details> at
       the bottom of the tab. Nobody cross-references a legend while reading.
       dePseudonymize() now substitutes real names AT RENDER TIME, in the browser,
       against a legend that was built in the browser after the response came
       back. The prompt is untouched and still carries pseudonymous ids only.
       Privacy on the wire, readability on the screen.
       It is tolerant of how a model actually writes ids back - it drops the
       "Account-" prefix constantly, so the bare form is matched too - and it
       substitutes longest-id-first so "Account-BEST1" can never eat the "BEST11"
       sitting in the same sentence.

    2. THE LENGTH CAP WAS TOO LOW, AND SAID NOTHING WHEN IT BIT. maxTokens 2000
       truncated the fourth section on every run: the NEXT lens ended mid-sentence
       ("outreach now - 70 days silent,") with nothing on screen explaining why.
       Four sections of 60-120 words plus a model's natural preamble does not fit
       in 2000. Raised to 3200, and the last section now carries an explicit
       notice when the cap is still reached. The rerun returned untruncated.

    3. EVERYTHING ELSE HELD. The four-section parse, the pseudonym legend, the
       monotonic request guard and the no-key degradation all behaved exactly as
       built. The output itself was specific and evidence-bound - it cited session
       counts, silence in days and unanswered-attempt counts, and did not invent a
       single fact that was not in the prompt.

    TESTED ON AN ISOLATED ORIGIN, deliberately. The demo book and the API key live
    on 127.0.0.1:8801; the real book lives on :8800 and has no key configured at
    all. Different origins mean different localStorage, so the real book was never
    within reach of the demo seed at any point. Verified afterwards: 37 accounts,
    41 activities, byte-stable storage, and no demo account names present.

    The key itself was never handled, transmitted or read by anything but the
    user's own browser talking to their own provider.

    Three checks, 295 -> 298.

  v95 - What to do next stops doing bookkeeping.

    The Gainsight batches moved to Today at v94, and this chapter was left
    rendering them anyway - which made the one chapter named for judgement the one
    chapter doing admin. They are gone from it entirely.

    It now answers three things per account: WHY this needs you, WHAT the act is,
    and - where the evidence names one - WHICH SPECIALIST to bring in. Onboarding
    failures are rarely solved by an OSM working harder. They are solved by
    routing to whoever can actually clear the thing, at the moment the evidence
    supports it.

    ROUTING IS EVIDENCE-BASED, NEVER A GUESS. The question the customer actually
    wrote is what names the specialist:
      - carbon accounting, factors, consolidation, CSRD  -> Climate Consultant
      - roadmap, defect, limitation, workaround          -> Product Success Manager
      - "how was our workspace configured"               -> Partner implementation team
      - integrations, API, connector, SSO, pipeline      -> Solution Architect
      - a PATTERN of concerning language across notes    -> Account Executive
    The Account Executive is new to OSM_ESCALATION_PATHS. It fires on a pattern
    only, never on a single mention. And the route reads the EVIDENCE rather than
    the headline: the first draft keyed off rec.key, so an account with escalating
    language AND a flagged note got no route at all, because the flagged note
    outranks it in getRecommendedAction().

    THE EARLY CSM HANDOFF, on two roads, both requiring evidence:
      GHOSTED - quiet past the neglect threshold AND a re-engagement play already
      run and closed as "no effect". Quiet is a state; chased-and-still-quiet is a
      conclusion, and the plays layer added at v88 is the only place in this file
      that records an ATTEMPT rather than an outcome.
      MATURE - multi-solution (more than one curriculum component, which is what
      buying additional solutions looks like in this data), 80% of sessions
      complete, health 80 or better, on schedule, and owing the customer nothing.
      The first draft asked for 60% and health 70 and fired on SEVEN OF TWELVE
      accounts in a healthy book. A recommendation that applies to most of the
      portfolio is a background colour, not a recommendation.

    Unanswered questions on the card back carry a "Draft with AI" button reaching
    the same v70 generator the beat panel uses - it writes the draft onto the
    thread and says out loud that a human must read it before sending.

    "STRATEGIC" IS NOW "PATTERNS ACROSS ACCOUNTS", and it explains itself. It is
    the opposite of the per-account column: places where THREE OR MORE accounts
    are independently stuck at the same session, or weakest in the same readiness
    domain, so that one prepared run or one piece of content covers all of them.
    Below three it stays quiet, because small samples lie loudly.

    ALSO FIXED: getGainsightQueue() CREATED DATA.gainsightQueue as a side effect
    of being read - the exact defect fixed in getDayClose() at v90, sitting in a
    second getter. Only the writer creates it now. And a v73 check pinned the
    literal column labels "Administrative" and "Strategic"; it now asserts the
    two-column STRUCTURE instead, because a check that pins copy fails every time
    the copy improves.

    Seven checks, 288 -> 295.

  v94 - The read opens on a door, and the admin goes where the work is.

    THE LANDING. Copilot opened straight into five chapters of dense reading every
    single time, and the first thing an OSM does at 8am is not read five chapters.
    It now lands on the band, one computed sentence about the book the read is
    about, and a door.

    THE DOOR IS FREE, and this was the decision that shaped the whole pass. It
    never checks for an API key. A tab that goes blank when a key expires, or when
    the provider is down, is a tab you stop trusting - and the deterministic
    chapters were always the thing worth trusting. Verified by clicking Generate
    with no key configured: the read failed, said so in one line, and all five
    chapters still rendered.

    AI ENRICHES, IT DOES NOT GATE. One pseudonymized call returns four labelled
    lenses - WEEK, MONTH, BUILDS, NEXT - written from a customer success,
    implementation and project-management perspective, each landing inside its own
    chapter above the deterministic content. ONE call rather than four, because
    four would each reason from a slice of the book and contradict each other
    about the same account. Section parsing is deliberately tolerant of the ways a
    model actually formats headings ("## WEEK", "**WEEK**", a bare word), and a
    response that does not parse is shown WHOLE rather than silently dropped.

    THE WEEK IS NOW THE CALLS. callsToPrepare() returns the accounts you have
    actually booked inside a window, in date order, each card carrying what you
    said last time and every question they are still waiting on you for - typed
    and resolved on the card back. The month chapter takes the calls that fall
    beyond this week but inside the month. With nothing booked, the chapter says
    so plainly rather than letting an empty list imply a quiet week.

    THE STANDING ADMIN MOVED TO TODAY. A committed graduation date, the Success
    Outcome Plan, the CSAT contact and the readiness assessment are record-keeping
    - they belong where the work happens, not on the tab you visit to think. Four
    rails on Today, one per field, each listing exactly who is missing it: 49 gaps
    across the real book. IMPLEMENTATION ACCOUNTS ARE EXCLUDED, which is the v52
    ruling rather than a new one - those gates do not open until Go-Live, and
    nagging a partner-led build for them would be asking for something that does
    not exist yet. Copilot's Gainsight batches still show that a gate batches
    across several accounts; they no longer offer a second place to edit the same
    four fields.

    ALSO: the band rail collapses when nothing lands in the window. It was
    reserving 88px for marks it did not have, which on the new landing pushed the
    brief and the door below the fold - the two things the tab now opens with.

    Seven checks, 281 -> 288.

  v93 - The questions nobody answered, and three defects behind one complaint.

    THE QUESTIONS. An unanswered customer question was the most invisible thing in
    this file. It lived on the activity that raised it, it rendered in exactly one
    place - the beat side-panel on Today - and it had no entry in
    TODAY_SOURCE_DEFS and none in SIGNAL_DEFS. So it could never RANK an account
    into the day: you saw it only if you were already standing on that account for
    some other reason. An account whose only problem was that you owed the
    customer an answer was, by construction, silent.

    Now there is an "Owed an answer" deck at the top of the week chapter, ordered
    OLDEST QUESTION FIRST because an eleven-day-old question is worse than three
    asked this morning. The answer is typed on the card back, and "Mark answered"
    writes the same thread record the debrief box owns - one definition, two
    places to reach it. A new ASKED field joins the band telemetry.

    It is deliberately still NOT in the ranked day. Whether an unanswered question
    should outrank a stall is a real ruling about priority and it should be made
    on purpose, not arrive as a side effect of making questions visible.

    THREE DEFECTS BEHIND ONE REPORT. "I cannot see what customer or the
    information from the front of the card. Theres no way to go back even if you
    click done." Reproduced in the browser, and it was three separate things -
    none of them the flip toggle, which worked correctly in isolation:

      1. THE BACK NEVER SAID WHO. The flipped face carried the editor title, the
         fields and a Done button, and no account name at all. On a rail of
         thirteen near-identical cards that is the difference between making a
         correction and making a mistake. The back now carries the account name
         and the same context line the front shows.

      2. THE RAIL FORGOT ITS PLACE. Every save re-renders the whole app, and the
         rail snapped back to the first card each time. Measured: scrollLeft 424
         before the render, 0 after. So editing the ninth account left you looking
         at the first, with nothing to indicate what had become of the one you
         were on. Scroll position now lives in ui.deckState beside the flip state,
         and is restored with scroll-behavior forced to auto so it does not
         animate as though the user had just dragged there.

      3. THE CARD COULD VANISH MID-EDIT. Committing a graduation date removes that
         account from the "No date" list, which is correct behaviour and
         completely indistinguishable from the application losing your work. Any
         card-back write that moves a card out of its list now names it:
         "Howmet Aerospace Inc: graduation date set to Jan 2, 2027. It has moved
         out of this list."

    Six checks, 275 -> 281.

  v92 - Still building: the half of the book nobody was watching.

    Seventeen of thirty-seven accounts are in partner-led implementation. The v67
    ruling deliberately keeps them out of SIGNAL_DEFS so they are not chased for
    sessions and Gainsight gates they do not have yet - that ruling was right and
    stands. What it did NOT intend is what actually happened: nearly half the book
    became invisible, and one build had been silent for 196 days with nothing
    anywhere saying so.

    A new Copilot chapter, "Still building", placed straight after the month.
    That placement is the point: the "nothing lands in this window" sentence sits
    there, and the honest next sentence is that half the book is upstream of the
    runway entirely. That sentence now redirects into the chapter instead of
    trailing off into "spend it on the curriculum".

    NOTIFY, DO NOT NAG - the distinction is the entire design:
      - It NOTIFIES through two new band telemetry fields, BUILDING and QUIET,
        which are read every time the tab is opened. A readout, not a task.
      - It does NOT NAG: no TODAY_SOURCE_DEFS entry, no SIGNAL_DEFS entry, no tab
        badge. An implementation account still reaches the ranked day only through
        a commitment or a blocker, exactly as v67 ruled. A check asserts that no
        implementation source or signal has appeared.

    THE THRESHOLD IS ITS OWN NUMBER. config.implQuietDays, default 35, floor 14,
    editable in Settings, and deliberately NOT shared with neglectDays. Twenty-one
    days of silence from an account in onboarding is a problem; from a partner-led
    build it is a Tuesday. One number covering both populations would be wrong for
    both, and a check asserts the two are not the same value.

    THE GO-LIVE PROBLEM, AND THE RULING ON IT. Sixteen of seventeen builds have no
    target go-live date, so "late" is uncomputable for 94% of them. It is NOT
    added as a fourth readiness-strip gap: that strip shows one gap at a time in
    priority order over the LIVE book, and an implementation gap queued behind
    no_sessions (13 accounts) would never surface. Different population, different
    queue. It is the chapter's own honesty line instead, with a card back that
    sets it in context.

    And the chapter WORKS WITHOUT IT, which is why it could ship now: phaseHistory
    and startedOn are populated on all seventeen accounts, so days-in-phase
    carries the chapter on day one. Days-in-phase reads phaseHistory rather than
    the build start date - an account that reached UAT last week is not 200 days
    into UAT because the build began in January.

    ALSO FIXED - the v90 regression in "What to do next". Two equal columns was
    right when those columns held wrapping rows of name chips, and wrong once they
    held card rails: a 380px card inside a 546px column shows one card and a
    sliver, and showing the SHAPE of a list is the only reason a rail exists.
    Administrative and Strategic now stack at full width.

    Seven checks, 267 -> 275.

  v91 - The stack becomes a rail.

    v90's deck was CardSwap's fanned 3D stack: one card readable, the rest behind
    it. It worked, but it HID THE BOOK - you could not see the shape of a
    thirteen-account list without clicking through it one card at a time. This is
    the parallax carousel from the SwiftUI and bundui references instead: a
    horizontal rail of readable cards, snapping to centre, where each card's inner
    layer moves OPPOSITE to that card's distance from the middle of the rail.

    The arithmetic is the reference's, kept intact:
      parallax = -diff / railWidth * strength * cardWidth
    where diff is the card's centre minus the rail's centre. Snap-to-centre is
    native CSS scroll-snap - what the SwiftUI version gets from
    .scrollTargetBehavior(.viewAligned) - and costs no JavaScript.

    HORIZONTAL, NOT VERTICAL, and the reason is structural rather than aesthetic:
    the Copilot chapters already scroll vertically. A vertical carousel inside
    them would put two competing vertical scrolls in one column, and the one you
    wanted would never be the one that moved.

    THREE DEVIATIONS, all from the same root - the references parallax an IMAGE
    and this parallaxes a FIGURE:
      1. The moving layer carries a real number, picked by the deck's reason for
         existing: days to projected graduation on a graduation-date deck, open
         risks on a risk deck, weakest domain on a readiness deck, sessions
         completed everywhere else. Same rule every instrument in this file has
         had to meet since v79 - it must read something the book already computes,
         or it is decoration.
      2. The offset is CLAMPED to 18% of the card width. Unclamped, the figure
         slid clean out of its own card and belonged to nothing. An oversized
         image absorbs a large offset by revealing more of itself; a numeral
         cannot.
      3. Motion off, reduced motion, or JS half-loaded leaves a plain horizontal
         list of readable cards with no transform at all.

    RULE 0 AMENDED, DELIBERATELY AND IN THE OPEN. It said "nothing else may read
    scroll position", and a parallax reads scroll position. But that rule was
    written after eleven devices all read WINDOW scroll - ambient motion happening
    under you while you try to read. A rail that parallaxes off its own scrollLeft
    is not that: you are dragging it directly, it is the only thing moving, and it
    stops when your finger does. The header amendment names the distinction rather
    than pretending the rule was never broken. Two checks hold the line: the
    parallax must read the RAIL and never the window, and rail listeners must be
    torn down before each rebind, because attachEvents() runs on every render and
    a scroll listener added without teardown stacks one copy per repaint.

    The flip survives: one open card per rail, remembered in ui.deckState, and
    opening a second card closes the first - two records open at once gives no
    signal for which one you are editing.

    Two checks, 265 -> 267.

  v90 - The card deck, and the AI chapter deleted.

    Copilot listed accounts as rows of name chips. A chip tells you an account is
    implicated and then makes you leave the tab to do anything about it - which is
    how a page about "the read on your whole book" turns into a launcher for nine
    round trips. Every list is now a DECK: one account at a time, readable, with
    the fix for the thing that put it in that list on the BACK of its own card.
    Flip, fill it in, move on. The flyout is still one click away, and it lands on
    the tab that matches the card rather than always on Overview.

    FIVE BACKS, one per reason an account can appear: graduation date, Success
    Outcome Plan, CSAT contact, readiness assessment, and risk verdicts. Which
    back a deck carries is decided by WHY the deck exists - the month chapter
    hands out graduation dates, the Gainsight batches hand out the gate they are
    batching, the portfolio lens and the weekly diff hand out risk.

    ONE DEFINITION PER WRITE. toggleComplianceGate / setOutcomeNumber /
    setCsatContact / setSentimentAnswer / setTargetGradDate were extracted from
    bindBeatPane()'s event handlers, which is where they had been trapped. The
    beat panel now calls them too. Two copies of "mark this gate done" drift, and
    the newer copy always wins silently.

    THE ANIMATION, and what was and was not taken. The stack geometry is
    CardSwap's (react-bits): fanned slots at x i*distX, y -i*distY, z
    -i*distX*1.5, z-index descending, and a promote that moves every card forward
    one slot on a stagger. NOT taken:
      - the timing. CardSwap runs 2s elastic by default and 0.8s on its power
        profile; rule 0 caps this file at 500ms, so 420ms with a 45ms stagger.
      - the auto-advance timer. Right for a landing page showing off, wrong for a
        work surface: a card that moves on its own while you are reading it is a
        card you lose your place in. This one moves only when you move it.
      - the uniform skew. It shears the sentence you are being asked to read and
        the field you are being asked to fill in, so it is a depth cue on the
        cards BEHIND and never on the one in front.
    No GSAP. One transform per card per interaction plus a CSS transition, the
    same one-scalar model the band already uses.

    FLIP STATE LIVES IN ui.deckState, not in a CSS class. Every save re-renders
    the whole app, so a flip held only in the DOM would be thrown away the moment
    you edited anything on the card - which is the one thing the card is for.

    AI CHAPTER REMOVED. It was a permanently-empty section whose entire body was
    an apology for being empty, sitting at the end of a tab that is supposed to be
    a read on the book. The FEATURE stays: Generate is still on the band, where
    what you are asking it to read is the stretch of time it covers, and its
    output now appears directly under the band only when there is output. The
    data-egress disclosure moves with it and stays body copy - the one thing that
    must never become fine print is what leaves the browser.

    THREE BUGS FOUND WHILE TESTING, TWO OF THEM OLDER THAN THIS VERSION:
      1. Risk log keys are themselves composite, so the verdict button's
         split on the pipe truncated the key, the hub lookup missed, and the
         button was a silent no-op that reported success. Now splits on the first
         delimiter only, pinned by a check that asserts the naive split fails.
      2. getDayClose() CREATED DATA.dayClose as a side effect of being read, and
         dayIsClosed() runs on every Today render - so merely looking at the tab
         added a key to the book. Latent since v73 and invisible until a freshly
         imported export made the key start absent. Reads no longer create; only
         closeTheDay() does.
      3. The v73 day-close check restored by assigning the previous value back,
         and assigning undefined CREATES the key. Same class of bug as (2).

    Also added buildRiskHubList({readOnly:true}), so the card backs can list what
    there is to action without stamping first-seen dates on every risk in the
    book. Without it, opening Copilot silently started every risk's clock.

    Eleven checks, 255 -> 265.

  v89 - The Gainsight CSV import, and the largest remaining gap closed.

    THE GAP. Every ranked list in this file sorted on EXECUTION QUALITY ALONE. A
    $2M account and a $40k account with the same health ranked identically, and
    the tool would send you to the smaller one on a fraction of severity. "Where
    do I spend my hour" is the only question this product exists to answer, and
    it was answering it with half the information.

    customer.tier / customer.arr / customer.renewalDate, filled by Settings ->
    Import from Gainsight (CSV), or typed on Edit Account. Hand-typed values go
    through the SAME parser as the import, so "250k" means one thing.

    THE RULING ON VALUE: it BREAKS TIES INSIDE A SEVERITY BAND and never crosses
    one. A burning small account still outranks a healthy large one - urgency is
    about what is on fire, value is about which fire to walk to first. A tool
    that let ARR outrank severity would quietly teach an OSM to neglect the
    accounts least able to survive neglect. The order is now:
      severity desc -> health asc -> VALUE desc -> staleness desc -> name -> id
    With no ARR anywhere every weight is equal, the term is always zero, and the
    v61 order is preserved bit for bit. The feature turns itself on as values
    arrive: no toggle, no behaviour migration, nothing to remember.

    CSV IS PARSED IN THIS FILE. The existing sheet importers go through XLSX from
    a CDN and fail with "check your internet connection" - in a document whose
    whole promise is that it opens from file://. parseCsv() implements the RFC
    4180 grammar that actually appears in exports: quoted fields, embedded
    commas, doubled quotes, CRLF, newlines inside a field, and a leading BOM.
    Offline, and asserted rather than trusted.

    MATCHING IS TWO-TIER AND SAYS WHICH FIRED. EXACT ignores punctuation and
    case, so "Airbnb, Inc." and "Airbnb Inc" are one account and no judgement is
    required. LOOSE additionally drops legal suffixes ("Crowdstrike" for
    "Crowdstrike Holdings, Inc."), which IS a judgement call, so it is labelled
    in the review for a human to check. A loose key that collides between two
    accounts is reported AMBIGUOUS and skipped, never guessed. Collapsing all of
    this into one silent match is how an import writes a million-dollar renewal
    date onto the wrong account.

    RENEWAL PRESSURE, GATED. A new SIGNAL_DEFS entry fires when the projected
    runway lands at or after renewal - the sentence the tracker could never say.
    It returns null when paceIsFallback(), because firing a commercial alarm off
    an assumed projection is exactly what v87 exists to stop. Measured on the
    real book with ten renewal dates loaded: NINE correctly silenced, one fired.
    Without the gate that is nine false alarms on day one.

    SCHEMA 9 -> 10, inferring nothing. There is no mapping from `module` to a
    tier, no way to guess a contract value, and no renewal date hiding in the old
    shape. An unreadable value is DELETED, never coerced to zero - a zero would
    sort that account to the bottom of every tie-break in the product.

    Eleven checks, 240 -> 255.

  v88 - Plays, and the question the tracker never asked.

    PLAYS. SIGNAL_DEFS detected a condition and getRecommendedAction() returned
    ONE sentence. But the things that actually go wrong in onboarding are
    sequences: an account going quiet is reach the champion, reach around them,
    reach the sponsor, decide - spread over two weeks. The failure mode is never
    "I didn't know", it is "I did step one and then the week ate it".

    PLAYBOOK_DEFS holds five plays (re-engagement, pace recovery, champion
    change, unblock, graduation readiness). A matching signal SUGGESTS one, a
    human STARTS it, and its steps carry dates measured from the start date -
    so a play begun late is late in its own terms rather than instantly overdue.
    Overdue steps surface on Today at severity 3 and dispatch to a new Plays tab.

    Two rules, each pinned by a check:
      1. NOTHING AUTO-STARTS. A tool that opens work on your behalf teaches you
         to close things without reading them, and then the one that mattered
         gets closed too.
      2. CLOSING CARRIES A VERDICT - worked, no effect, no longer relevant. A
         closed play stays on the account, because "we tried re-engagement in
         June and it did nothing" is the most useful thing to know before
         starting another one.

    DID IT HOLD? The tracker measured onboarding all the way to graduation and
    then stopped caring, which meant it could tell you that you FINISHED but
    never whether it WORKED. Graduating now schedules one question at +30 days
    (editable in Settings): are they still doing the thing you onboarded them
    onto? Three answers, because five would be false precision on a judgement
    call. graduationDurability() is the only figure in this file that measures
    the OUTCOME of the work rather than its volume - and it returns null rather
    than 0% when nothing has been reviewed, because an empty denominator is the
    same class of lie as a band plotting an empty axis.

    Hooked into changeStage() rather than the graduate button, so a graduation
    set from the Kanban board, a bulk edit or an import correction all get the
    same follow-up. Re-graduating never erases an answer already given.

    SCHEMA 8 -> 9: customer.playbooks, customer.gradReview. The migration
    deliberately opens no play and schedules no review for accounts that
    graduated before this version existed - both would be the tracker inventing
    history it never observed. Old books arrive with neither, which is the truth
    about them.

    Fourteen checks, 225 -> 240.

  v87 - Onboarding, and it is not a welcome tour.

    THE FINDING: this app's empty states are good and almost never seen. "No
    accounts yet" is a one-morning condition. The state it is actually in - and
    stays in - is THIN: accounts present, nothing fed. On the real book, 30 of 32
    live accounts had no completed session, 15 of 15 had nothing booked, and 14
    had no logged activity at all. In that state the app said nothing. It
    rendered pace, projections, coverage and due dates with total composure, all
    of them computed from DATA.config.defaultPaceDays.

    So the onboarding job here is not to teach the product. It is to make the
    product admit what it cannot yet tell you and hand over the control that
    fixes it. bookReadiness() names ONE gap at a time, in the order that unblocks
    the most: sessions feed pace, pace feeds every projection; bookings feed
    coverage; notes feed every signal. Dismissals are per gap and permanent, and
    live in the browser rather than the book - a preference about being nagged is
    not a fact about a customer.

    THE HONESTY HALF: a projected graduation date with no completed sessions
    behind it now reads "Assumed", dashed, with a title explaining why, instead

  v80 - The showcase techniques that survive contact with an ops tool. Plus a switch.

    Two references this pass: a WebGL agency showreel and a paid GSAP effects
    library. NEITHER PORTS, and it is worth saying why rather than half-doing it.
    The showreel is a single full-viewport canvas with a preloader that was still
    at 6% twelve seconds in, across seventeen screens whose entire job is to be
    looked at. The library assumes GSAP, ScrollTrigger and usually Lenis. This
    file is one self-contained document with no build step that opens from
    file://, and it is opened twenty times a day between customer calls. A canvas
    preloader and a hundred kilobytes of animation library are the wrong tax on
    that.

    But most of what makes those sites feel expensive is not the WebGL. Four
    techniques carry it, and all four cost nothing:

      VELOCITY SKEW - the deck and the chapter column lean with how fast the page
      is moving and settle when it stops. One smoothed, clamped scalar written
      once a frame into a CSS variable, which is the same model the Day already
      uses.
      LINE-MASK REVEALS - chapter headings rise from behind a clipping edge
      instead of fading. The wrapper is added after render, so the markup the
      checks read is untouched and the text stays selectable.
      MAGNETIC HOVER - rail rows and the gate buttons lean toward the cursor,
      bounded to a few pixels. A control that wanders further stops being where
      you aimed.
      INERTIAL SETTLE on the deck.

    AND A SWITCH, which is the part I would argue for hardest. Every pass this
    session has added motion to a surface someone uses under time pressure.
    Settings now carries Motion: Full or Calm. Calm keeps the deck, the parallax
    and the instruments — the structure is not a flourish — and drops the skew,
    the reveals and the magnets. It is per browser, never in the book, and
    prefers-reduced-motion overrides it in the safe direction: a switch that could
    turn motion ON against the OS setting would be worse than no switch at all.

    THE CHECK THAT MATTERS is not that the switch works, it is that NOTHING
    DEPENDS ON AN ANIMATION HAVING RUN. Every rule that hides or moves a heading
    has to sit behind .fx-on, so with the class absent the page is still, not
    blank. That is asserted by walking the live stylesheet, and the mask is
    verified to preserve its text exactly.

    THE CHECK CAUGHT ITSELF TWICE, both false positives worth recording because
    they are the same class of bug: ".cp3-h" prefix-matched ".cp3-horizon", and
    "opacity: 0.8" matched a naive /opacity:\s*0/. A visible hairline was being
    reported as hidden content. Both halves needed a boundary.

  v74.1 - Calendar self-checks, and the defect they found.

    The day timeline and the time normalizer had been covered since v70/v72. What
    had no check at all was everything to do with dates LEAVING the tool -
    buildSessionIcs(), the .ics the OSM drops into Google Calendar. It is the one
    artefact this tracker produces that lands in somebody else's system, and a
    malformed one fails at the moment it is imported, which is after it was sent.

    THE DEFECT: NO LINE FOLDING. RFC 5545 section 3.1 caps a content line at 75
    octets. A realistic account name - "Koninklijke Philips Electronics North
    America Corporation" - produced a 192-octet SUMMARY and a 138-octet
    DESCRIPTION. Google tolerates over-long lines; stricter parsers and some mail
    gateways do not, and a rejected invite is indistinguishable from an invite the
    customer ignored.

    icsFoldLine() folds at 75 OCTETS with continuation lines starting one space.
    Two things it has to get right that a slice(0,75) does not:
      - UTF-8 length, not character count. The session titles carry an em dash,
        which is 3 octets, so a character-counted fold overruns the limit anyway.
      - It must never split a character. Iterating with for..of walks code points,
        so a multi-byte character - or an emoji in a customer name - cannot be cut
        into two invalid bytes.
    Folding is deliberately the LAST step: escape, then fold. The other order can
    place a fold between a backslash and the character it escapes.

    SEVEN CHECKS ADDED, 185 -> 192:
      - the invite is RFC 5545 clean: CRLF only, one VEVENT, every line <= 75
        octets, and unfolding is LOSSLESS rather than merely producing short lines
      - a comma, semicolon, backslash or pasted newline in an account name cannot
        forge a calendar property. Names arrive from imports and free text; this
        is how a calendar file becomes a parse error, or silently gains a field
        nobody typed
      - an all-day invite ends the NEXT day (DTEND is exclusive), verified across
        a month end, a year end, and into and out of a leap day
      - date arithmetic is reversible and never drifts: isoAddDays is the only
        date-shifting primitive in the tracker - pacing, projections, pause
        expiry, the ICS end date and the fit window all run through it
      - the invite date prefers what you scheduled, then the pace, then today,
        and is always a real ISO date whichever branch it took
      - noon and midnight print correctly. 12-hour formatting fails at exactly
        those two points, and the day timeline prints them beside every call
      - today's calls run in clock order, with untimed ones sorted after rather
        than dropped or floated to the top

    WHAT WAS ALREADY RIGHT, and is now held in place: escaping of commas,
    semicolons, backslashes and newlines; CRLF line endings; exclusive DTEND; and
    the local-component date formatting that keeps isoAddDays from shifting a day
    in UTC+ zones.

    Every one of the seven was then deliberately falsified - folding removed,
    escaping removed, DTEND made inclusive, date arithmetic drifted, noon
    misformatted, the scheduled date ignored, clock times dropped from the sort.
    Each fault was caught by its target check, and removing folding trips exactly
    one check and nothing else.

    192/192, four consecutive runs, risk memory untouched.
