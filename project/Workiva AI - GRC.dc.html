the one idea to teach

Workiva AI is most useful when you give it a real audit or risk task, the controls, narrative, or evidence it should read, and a clear picture of what a useful answer looks like.

Quick wins come from tasks that are repetitive and text-heavy: rewriting control language, mapping frameworks, reviewing evidence. Your job isn't to teach "AI" — it's to find one of those tasks in the customer's audit cycle and remove part of it during the call.

the shift

software that helps → software that does the work

Enterprise software has always run on one model: you do the work and the software helps by organizing, connecting, and speeding things up. AI breaks that model. Software can now understand what needs to happen and do it.

old model

The customer’s team decides in a meeting that a new framework needs to be mapped against the control set. That becomes a weeks-long action item.

new model

The software is already mapping controls, listing unmapped requirements, and drafting the gap summary before the meeting ends.

the line that never moves

traceable. governed. defensible.

More automation means more decisions about how much control to hand over, and the customer makes that choice. Self-driving on a familiar route in good weather feels fine; on an icy mountain road you want the wheel back. Every AI action explains itself, respects permissions, and holds up to an audit. That isn’t a constraint on the AI story. It is the AI story.

“More of the work gets done, you stay in control, and you can trust the result.” Julie Iskow, President & CEO

Means nothing on its own. Add the number of controls, which are key, which frameworks each maps to, which failed last cycle, and suddenly it can be analyzed, explained, and questioned. Workiva AI is moving from the value in a cell to understanding what that number is, where it came from, and what it connects to. That understanding is what lets it take on more of the work well, not just faster.

what
workiva ai is

A secure, integrated assistant built into the Workiva platform. Not a separate chatbot you paste into — it reads the control matrix, process narrative, or evidence file you're already working in.

three places it shows up

point to these in a demo

Global chat

Available anywhere in the platform, including mobile. Ask questions, brainstorm, attach reference files (PDF, DOCX) for analysis, and iterate turn by turn.

Best for: "what regulations impact our controls?", first drafts, working through a problem.

File companions

A panel inside Documents and Spreadsheets that reads the current file. Select a table or paragraph, then edit inline, generate content, or use pre-built quick edits.

Best for: rewriting control statements, drafting test procedures, summarizing risk tables.

Flowchart visualizer

In the GRC solution, paste a process narrative such as "accounts payable workflow" and the AI generates a process flow diagram you can link risks and controls to.

Best for: process documentation, walkthroughs, visualizing where controls sit.

the three-question discovery pattern
1 · What takes time?

"What is something in this workflow you repeatedly spend 20 to 60 minutes doing?"

2 · What is the source of truth?

"Where is the information you use to do it: the control matrix, a process narrative, uploaded evidence, the framework, a policy, or last year’s workpapers?"

3 · What does "done" look like?

"When you’re finished, what have you produced: a rationalized RCM, a coverage gap list, a test plan, a remediation plan, an audit committee summary?"

Those three answers almost directly populate Source, Context, Objective, and Parameters in SCOPE. And whenever a pain surfaces, don't stop at the outcome: ask "walk me through how you do that today." That one question separates a vague pain from an investigable workflow.

what a successful ai moment looks like

Real customer task + real source context + visible output + one refinement + repeat-use discussion.

Better than a CRM field that says "Workiva AI discussed." Weight first real task and repeat use most. Exposure isn't behavior change.

the moment to aim for

An internal auditor selects the AP controls in their own RCM, gets a list of overlapping pairs with control IDs, asks for key controls only, and says "we could use this every testing cycle."

what drives adoption

Freedom to experiment, role-specific applications, and a prompt library they can start from. Not abstract AI mastery.
