What the Data Suggests May Have Driven the Increase
Air freight (+$58K, +96.7%) — The single largest driver. The source data indicates $45K of the increase relates to expedited air shipping for a one-time customer recovery event, which may explain the majority of this line's spike. The remaining ~$13K increase above Q2 levels is unexplained by the note.
Fuel surcharge (+$14K, +36.8%) — A carrier surcharge rate increase from 9% to 12% effective August 1 may account for this rise. This appears to be a structural cost change (rate-driven), not a volume effect.
Ground freight (+$22K, +5.4%) — A moderate increase with no explanatory note. This could hypothetically reflect volume growth or rate changes, but no data supports either conclusion.
Warehousing (+$1K, +0.8%) — Essentially flat; not a meaningful driver.

Note on the one-time item: If the $45K expedited air event is truly non-recurring, underlying freight costs may be closer to ~$678K — roughly a 7.9% increase quarter-over-quarter — once normalized.
